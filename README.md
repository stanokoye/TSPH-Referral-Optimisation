# Hospital Referral Process Optimisation
### The Sydney Private Hospital, Ashfield NSW 2131

> **End-to-end Business Analysis engagement — stakeholder mapping, process analysis, 3-track triage design, and a full business case for a Sydney private hospital.**
> Based on publicly available information and direct observation.

---

## 📋 Project Overview

The Sydney Private Hospital (TSPH) is an 88-bed private surgical and rehabilitation centre serving the Inner West of Sydney for over 90 years. Despite strong clinical capability, the hospital was losing referrals to competitor facilities due to a referral response time exceeding 2 hours — well above the 15-minute target set by the Director.

This project applies end-to-end Business Analysis techniques to identify the root causes of the delay, redesign the referral process, and build a business case for implementation.

---

## 🏥 The Business Problem

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Organisation** | The Sydney Private Hospital — Surgical & Rehabilitation |
| **Beds** | 88 beds · 8 operating theatres · 4 gyms · Hydrotherapy pool |
| **Referral volume** | ~100 referrals/month across 5 channels |
| **Current response time** | 2+ hours |
| **Target response time** | Under 15 minutes |
| **Problem** | Calls misdirected through general reception, manual bed checks, doctor unreachable by phone, no triage system, verbal/paper information capture |
| **Impact** | Est. 15 referrals lost/month → AUD $150,000 monthly revenue loss |

---

## 💡 The Solution — 3-Track Triage System

All referral channels (phone, email, fax, online portal, walk-in GP) funnel into a **Central Referral Queue** with priority given to phone calls. The Referral Coordinator screens each referral against published eligibility criteria and assigns one of three tracks:

| Track | Type | Volume | Target Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Track 1** | Rehab — screened against eligibility criteria, confirmed by NOM | 70% (~70/month) | Under 5 min |
| **Track 2** | Surgical — digital approval request to on-call doctor | 30% (~30/month) | Under 15 min |
| **Track 3** | Excluded — immediate decline at Coordinator level | ~5% | Under 2 min |

**Weighted average response time: ~7 minutes** — a 94% improvement on current state.

## 🗺️ Implementation Roadmap

| Phase | Timeline | Focus | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 — Quick win** | Weeks 1–4 | Coordinator appointment, triage protocol, staff training | Response time drops to 60–90 min — no tech investment |
| **Phase 2 — Core system** | Weeks 5–16 | Dedicated line, central queue, digital approval, portal | Response time under 15 min — all tracks operational |
| **Phase 3 — Integration** | Weeks 17–20 | Live bed dashboard, HIS auto-population, auto-confirmation | Zero manual entry — full audit trail |
| **Phase 4 — Optimise** | Weeks 21–24 | Power BI KPI dashboard, continuous improvement, scale | Director-level reporting and operational sustainability |

Delivered via 12 two-week Agile sprints across a 24-week program.

## ⚠️ Key Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Rapid referral volume increase post-improvement | Operational overload | Phase rollout + capacity planning |
| Single point dependency — Referral Coordinator | Process bottleneck | Cross-training + backup roster |
| Clinical resistance to new workflow | Adoption failure | Early engagement + pilot program |
| Technology integration delays | Timeline slippage | MVP approach before full integration |
| Over-reliance on digital doctor approval | Doctor fatigue | Escalation rules + prioritisation logic |

> **Primary risk is adoption and operational scalability — not technology.**
